1. The Limits of Prediction & Quantitative Models
The Inherent Unpredictability of Systemic Shocks

Source: Avery, A. (2020). Cybersecurity Scenario Modeling: Imagining the Black Swans for Digital Infrastructures Risk Management. SAIS 2020 Proceedings.

Idea: Qualitative frameworks capture risks outside quantiative frameworks suggesting feasibility of quantiative approach has low probability.


The Gap Between Modeled and Real-World Risk

Source: (Author). (2025). Integrating CTI and Threat Modeling for Cyber Resilience. PLOS ONE, 20(11).

Idea: Real time intelligence is difficult because most organizations separate threat modeling as a static diconnected system and core cyber threat activities have weak evaluation methods, ad hoc processes, and mostly manual.


2. Implementation & Measurement Challenges
The Persistent Challenge of Real-Time Response

Source: Kim, J., Lee, J., & Kwon, H. (2023). Cyber-Resilience Evaluation Methods Focusing on Response Time to Cyber Infringement. Sustainability, 15(18), 13404.

Idea: Difficult to counter attacks across complex systems quickly because real-time detection and response capabilities are lacking for most organizations.


The Lack of Consensus on Resilience Metrics

Source: (Authors). (2025). Measuring Cyber Resilience in Industrial IoT: A Systematic Literature Review. Journal of Management Analytics.

Idea: No agreed upon metrics for cyber resilience and most feel unprepared. Cyber Risk Index adoption will be difficult.


The Ambition of Engineering Antifragility

Source: Băleanu, V., Dinu, A., & Toth, L. (2023). How to Build Antifragility in Innovative Start-up Companies. Review of Managerial Science, 17, 633–662.

Idea: Empirical studies on antifragile systems are rare. Limited evidence that it is possible.
